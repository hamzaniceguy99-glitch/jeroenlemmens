How we work

A consultancy that names its refusals first

JEROEN LEMMENS SRL was created on 6 September 2010 and is registered with the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises under number 0829.093.345, with its registered office at 10 Schapenbaan in Meise, Flemish Brabant. The work is public relations and communication consultancy, together with business and management consultancy and related business support.

Belgian economic law contains a list of commercial practices treated as unfair in all circumstances, and three of them sit precisely where this profession operates. Using editorial content to promote a product that the trader has paid for, without the paid character being clear from the content itself. Falsely presenting yourself as a consumer, or creating the impression that you are not acting commercially. And submitting or commissioning consumer reviews, misrepresenting them, or claiming they come from real purchasers without reasonable steps to check. Because those practices are prohibited in all circumstances, nobody has to show that a consumer was actually misled and no commercial justification helps. That is a clearer line than most of this industry works to, and it rules out a familiar list of tactics. What is left is positioning that rests on evidence, media relations that distinguish earned coverage from paid placement, and crisis work prepared before the crisis. We are not a law practice and we give no legal advice, so anything carrying legal consequences goes to your own lawyer. And no consultancy can promise coverage, because the decision to publish belongs to an editor.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

Why start a pitch with what you will not do?

Because those three things are prohibited in all circumstances rather than weighed case by case, so there is genuinely nothing to negotiate. Undisclosed paid editorial, posing as a consumer, and writing or commissioning reviews are on the list of practices treated as unfair in every case, which means no one needs to prove a consumer was misled. Knowing that in the first meeting saves a client from asking in the third.

Everyone buys reviews. Is it really that serious?

It is on the blacklist, which is the serious end. Submitting false reviews or commissioning someone else to submit them, misrepresenting reviews or social endorsements, and claiming reviews come from actual purchasers without taking reasonable and proportionate steps to verify it are all prohibited outright. So we do not write them, buy them, incentivise them or quietly filter out the bad ones. If a client runs a review function, we advise on how to run it consistently with those rules.

Can you guarantee us coverage?

No, and neither can anyone else. The decision to publish, and the tone of what gets published, belong to the editor. Any arrangement that guarantees publication is paid placement, which is a legitimate product, and it has to be disclosed as paid inside the content. Our fees are never contingent on coverage appearing, because a fee that depends on a placement creates an interest in getting it at any cost.

An influencer campaign without the advertising label is possible, surely?

It is not. The commercial nature of a communication has to be identifiable by the person receiving it, and a disclosure buried elsewhere or implied by context does not satisfy that. If there is a commercial relationship, it is apparent in the publication itself. In practice the labelled campaigns perform about as well, because audiences work it out regardless and resent being handled.

Can you handle a crisis for us without involving lawyers?

We can prepare one with you, and we would rather do that before anything happens. But we are not a law practice, we are not enrolled at any bar and we give no legal advice. A statement answering an allegation can carry legal consequences in defamation, in contract, in disclosure obligations or in employment, so where it does, your own lawyer reads it first and we work alongside them. A response that is rhetorically strong and legally reckless is not a service.

What do you do with our press contacts and journalist lists?

Treat them as personal data, which is what they are. There has to be a lawful basis for holding them and information duties owed to the people on them. Those lists are not traded, not shared between clients, and not kept because they might be useful one day. Embargoes get the same treatment: a binding undertaking rather than a courtesy to be broken for a better slot.
